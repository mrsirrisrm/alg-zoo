Regions holding 99% of inputs, whole space

T = 2–3: a few big panes

The true boundaries cut cleanly through large regions and there is almost no red. Enumerating the regions and integrating each one beats random sampling by a factor of 10,000 or more.

T = 4–5: the crossover

The panes fragment and thin red slivers appear along the black lines: inputs so close to a tie that the network's version of the boundary misses the true one. From here on, sampling beats the region-by-region method.

T = 10: a mosaic

The challenge model (h = 16, T = 10) has about 600,000 regions covering 99% of inputs. A flat slice only shows the cuts that cross it, so it looks merely busy. Most of the growth is in dimensions no picture can show; the chart tracks it.

Width vs length

Region counts grow roughly like (h·T)T. Width adds more cuts through the same space; each extra step adds a dimension in which the cuts multiply. Compare the hand-built model: just as many regions, but its errors sit in one simple, exactly known place.
